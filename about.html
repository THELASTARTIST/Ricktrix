RICKTRIX maps the share-auto network Kolkata actually runs on — fares, stops and timing, kept current by the people who ride it daily.

How it started

9:04 AM, Howrah crossing

Late, and no idea which auto to take

A commuter stands at a crossing with a dead phone signal and three auto-rickshaws idling in three different directions. Nobody can say for certain which one reaches Salt Lake, or for how much. This exact scene repeats for millions of people, every single morning, across the city.

The pattern

The city already knows the answer — it's just not written down

Every regular rider knows a handful of routes by heart. Drivers know their stretch. But that knowledge never leaves the person who has it — there was no shared map of Kolkata's actual share-auto network, only word of mouth, repeated one stranger at a time.

What we built

RICKTRIX: the network, written down and kept current

We started collecting routes the way commuters already share them — from person to person — and put them on one map, with real fares and real timing. Every route is added and corrected by someone who has actually ridden it, so the map gets more accurate the more the city uses it.

Where this goes

Kolkata first — then every city that runs on share-autos

We're starting where the problem is most familiar to us. The plan from here is straightforward: keep the Kolkata map accurate, then bring the same map to the next city where a share-auto is still the fastest way across town.
